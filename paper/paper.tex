\begin{abstract}
The guidance hypothesis holds that guidance breeds dependence: the longer a
learner is guided, the larger its fall when guidance is withdrawn. We test
it, and the same question for a model of the body, in one soft
actor-critic learner on six muscle-driven bodies, two from MyoSuite and four
planar arms of one to four joints, given a frozen forward model learned
from reward-free babbling or a frozen teacher acting through a distillation
term, with matched seeds, an adversarial control for each prior and training
to a checked plateau. Replacing a withdrawn teacher by a frozen snapshot of the
student, which keeps the loss term but adds no information, separates what
withdrawal removes: the teacher's share of the immediate drop falls with how
long it was attached, the deleted term's does not. Once training has
converged, a learner that lost its teacher is never detectably worse than one
never guided, at any attachment duration, on either body, whether guidance
supplemented or replaced its own gradient; with a competent teacher it is
better, by more the longer it was guided. A withdrawn body model can leave the
learner short of one that kept it, never behind one that never had it. What
remains after withdrawal is a shortfall against keeping the prior, not
dependence. What the priors are worth is bound to their content: the babble
model beats model-free learning on four of six bodies and a random model on all
six, and a teacher is worth its competence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\IEEEPARstart{H}{umans} and animals acquire motor skills in a handful of
trials; deep reinforcement learning on muscle-actuated bodies can need hundreds
of millions of environment steps. Computational sensorimotor theory offers two
accounts of what a biological learner brings to a task that an agent trained
from scratch does not. One is an internal forward model of the body, predicting
the sensory consequences of motor commands \cite{wolpert1995,kawato1999} and
adapted in the intrinsic coordinates of joints and muscles \cite{shadmehr1994}.
The other is guidance: the guidance hypothesis
\cite{salmoni1984,schmidt1992,winstein1994} holds that augmented feedback
steers the learner toward the correct response while breeding a dependence on
it, so that the longer it is given the larger the fall when it is withdrawn.
Both are claims about a \emph{prior} in the developmental sense, something
present before task learning begins, and both can be instantiated for an
artificial learner, compared on equal terms, and, what human studies rarely
do, taken away in pieces.

We do that here. Under one soft actor-critic backbone on six muscle-driven
bodies, we add either a frozen forward model of the body, learned once from
reward-free motor babbling and used for short-horizon imagined rollouts, or a
frozen teacher policy acting through a distillation term. Each route is given
the adversarial control the other has: a randomly initialised model in the
same imagination machinery, a randomly initialised teacher in the same
distillation loss. Every condition has at least twelve matched seeds, every comparison
is a paired contrast on the seed, and training runs until the learning curves
have plateaued. We propose no algorithm; like other simulation studies of what
an endowment is worth to a learner \cite{sen2023} or of what removing a
contingency does \cite{spisak2026}, the claim is about what withdrawing a motor
prior costs, and secondarily about what the prior was worth.

\emph{The withdrawal cost decomposes, and what is left at convergence is not
dependence.} Zeroing a teacher's distillation weight deletes a loss term as well
as the teacher's information. Replacing the teacher by a frozen snapshot of the
student keeps the term but carries nothing further, and shows on both MyoSuite
bodies that the teacher's share of the immediate drop falls with attachment
while the deleted term's does not; where the drop grows with attachment, the
term is what grows. Measured as the literature measures dependence, the
withdrawn learner against one never guided, no withdrawal leaves a deficit once
training has converged, and with a competent teacher the withdrawn learner is
ahead. The body model behaves alike once its withdrawal is made single-factor:
purging the imagined buffer also doubles the real data per update, and with
that held fixed the loss is detected on the elbow at 12k ($+22$~mrad), where
the standard protocol detects nothing, and is not detected at 100k, while on the
finger it grows to $+15$~mm, the imagination forgone after withdrawal. On
neither body is the withdrawn learner worse than one that never had a model.

\emph{What the priors are worth is smaller and bound to their content.} At
convergence the babble-trained body model beats model-free learning on four of
the six bodies and is never detectably worse; it beats a random model on all
six, and the random model's harm grows with joint count. A teacher is worth its
competence: on the finger a teacher within 3.2~mm of doing nothing caps the
student 28~mm below model-free learning, and a competent teacher on the same
body makes the student better than every condition without it.

Every run of an earlier, unpublished 12k version of this study that has a
counterpart here is reproduced seed for seed; scoring at that fixed endpoint
would have changed what the body-model results detect on both MyoSuite bodies.
Code and data will be released on acceptance.

\section{Background and Related Work}

\textbf{The guidance hypothesis.} Salmoni, Schmidt, and Walter
\cite{salmoni1984} argued that frequent augmented feedback guides performance
during acquisition but degrades learning; the account was named the guidance
hypothesis \cite{schmidt1989} and generalised to practice conditions
\cite{schmidt1992}. High-frequency physical guidance gave the poorest
retention in one test \cite{winstein1994} and reduced knowledge of results
improved delayed retention in another \cite{winstein1990}, but a preregistered
meta-analysis finds no reliable benefit of reduced feedback frequency
\cite{mckay2022,marschall2007}. The hypothesis is a claim about
\emph{dependence}, the learner being worse after withdrawal than one never
guided or guided less, and its mechanism is that guidance \emph{substitutes}
for the learner's own error correction. Human studies have kept a feedback
channel while corrupting or removing its information
\cite{buekers1992,kitago2013,ploettner2025}, but none separates the
information from the channel as guidance is withdrawn. Computational models of
dependence on robotic assistance place it in an effort or slacking term of the
learner's own cost \cite{emken2007,casadio2012}.

\textbf{Teacher--student learning.} Privileged learning
\cite{chen2019lbc,lee2020} distils a teacher that sees state the deployed
policy will not into a student on the student's own rollouts
\cite{lee2020,miki2022}; RMA \cite{kumar2021} regresses the privileged latent
instead. Distillation may be the student's entire training, or an auxiliary
term annealed or cut to make the student independent
\cite{schmitt2018,agarwal2022}; weaning can collapse the student, which
Agarwal et al.\ \cite{agarwal2022} attribute, without a test, to the change of
objective. A term toward the learner's own earlier policy stabilises
fine-tuning \cite{li2023proto,luo2023}; supervised distillation uses the
student itself or a random network as the teacher to separate what the term
does from what the teacher knows \cite{yuan2020,sarnthein2023}, and compares
stopping distillation mid-way only with continuing it \cite{cho2019}; in
language models a teacher-free signal can match a noisy teacher
\cite{ding2026}. To our knowledge no study splits the cost of withdrawing a
teacher into lost information and a deleted loss term by retargeting the term
to the learner's own frozen snapshot, which is formally a trust region to an
old policy, used here as a counterfactual control. A cloned policy fine-tuned by
off-policy reinforcement often degrades at the transition, because a fresh or
miscalibrated critic gives unreliable values outside the data
\cite{nair2020,uchendu2023,zhang2023}; a retained cloning term is reported to
prevent this \cite{zhao2022}.

\textbf{Developmental robotics.} A body model built from self-generated
movement and a caregiver's guidance are the two routes this study instantiates.
Developmental robotics \cite{lungarella2003,asada2009,cangelosi2015} treats
motor babbling as a way to learn forward models of the self
\cite{dearden2005}, intrinsic motivation as what drives it
\cite{oudeyer2007}, and infant reaching as the paradigm case
\cite{thelen1993}. Scaffolding theory counts a tutor as successful when the
learner does the task alone after the tutor fades \cite{wood1976}; the
withdrawn learner against one never guided, at convergence, is that criterion
measured.

\textbf{Model-based reinforcement learning (RL) and body models.} World Models
\cite{ha2018} trains a model once from random rollouts; the Dreamer series
\cite{hafner2020,hafner2021,hafner2025} and TD-MPC2 \cite{hansen2024} keep the
model learning online; MBPO \cite{janner2019} motivates the short branched
rollouts of our Dyna step. Self-modelling \cite{bongard2006} and goal babbling
\cite{rolf2010} learn body models by directed exploration; Plan2Explore
\cite{sekar2020} and APV \cite{seo2022} pretrain a model without task reward
and reuse or fine-tune it; offline methods \cite{kidambi2020,yu2020,yu2021}
fit a model to logged, rewarded data and optimise conservatively under it. Most
muscle RL is model-free \cite{berg2023,chiappa2023,schumacher2023,wochner2022};
muscle world models are co-learned with the skill \cite{feng2023,kim2025}, and
frozen models learned from reward-free actuation already drive soft and
muscle-driven bodies by planning or model-only RL
\cite{berdica2024,wu2024modex,zheng2025}. We use such a model only for short
Dyna rollouts mixed with real data, price it at convergence, and control it with
a random model; that an inaccurate model can make model-based RL worse than
model-free, more so in higher dimension, is known
\cite{janner2019,voelcker2022}. MyoSuite \cite{caggiano2022} provides two of
the bodies.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Bodies, task and backbone}
\texttt{myoElbowPose1D6M} is a one-degree-of-freedom elbow driven by six
muscles; error is a joint-angle error in milliradians (mrad).
\texttt{myoFingerReachRandom-v0} is a four-joint index finger driven by five
muscles reaching a three-dimensional fingertip target; error is a Euclidean
fingertip error in millimetres (mm). Four planar arms of one, two, three and
four revolute joints, driven by two, four, eight and ten MuJoCo muscles
(antagonist pairs, biarticular from three joints on), reach a planar fingertip
target; error is in mm. Errors are never compared across bodies. The task is
reach; episodes are 100 steps from a fixed start pose; training draws from
eight fixed targets; evaluation is greedy on twenty disjoint held-out targets,
so every number here is a generalisation measurement (on the one-joint bodies
an interpolation test; Sec.~\ref{sec:limits}). One soft actor-critic (SAC)
\cite{haarnoja2018,haarnoja2018b} backbone is used in every condition: two
hidden layers of 128 units, update-to-data ratio~2, batch~128, 1{,}000 warm-up
steps, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, automatic entropy temperature.
Conditions differ only in what is added.

\subsection{The two priors}
The \emph{forward-model} route adds a body model $f_\theta(s,a)\mapsto s'$
trained once on task-agnostic, reward-free Ornstein--Uhlenbeck muscle
activations ($10^5$ transitions on the elbow, $2\times10^5$ on the finger and
on each planar arm), then frozen, and used for short-horizon Dyna
\cite{sutton1991} rollouts branched from sampled real states; the environment's
analytic reward is evaluated at the imagined states, so every reading of this
route is scoped to a forward model \emph{plus} a known reward function. Each
update draws 64 real and 64 imagined transitions where model-free conditions
draw 128 real.

The \emph{guidance} route adds a frozen teacher actor. The student's actor loss
is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\pi = q\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SAC}}
 + c\,\big\|\tanh\mu_{\mathrm{student}}(o) - \tanh\mu_{\mathrm{teacher}}(o)\big\|^2 ,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
on replayed observations $o$, with $c=1$ while the teacher is attached; the
critic trains on reward in every condition. In the \emph{additive} regime
($q=1$ always) the term supplements the student's own policy gradient, as
concurrent augmented feedback does; this is the coach used everywhere unless
stated. In the \emph{replacing} regime ($q=0$ while attached, $q=1$ from
withdrawal) the actor is moved only by the teacher until withdrawal, as
physical guidance moves the limb and as reward-free distillation
\cite{lee2020,miki2022} does.

Three teachers are used. T1 on each MyoSuite body is the terminal actor of a
model-free run of 30k rewarded steps on a seed outside the study's twelve; it
scores 27.2~mrad on the elbow's held-out targets, and 169.1~mm on the finger's,
where a zero-activation policy scores 172.3, so the finger T1 is within 3.2~mm
of doing nothing. T2 on the finger is the terminal actor of a body-model run of
100k steps on seed~1 of the study (35.6~mm held-out); it is used for every
finger withdrawal experiment and reported beside T1 in the core grid. T2
varies teacher quality and is not budget-matched to anything. One of the twelve
students shares its initialisation seed with the run that produced T2;
excluding that student changes no finger-T2 conclusion (every contrast keeps its
sign and detection).

\subsection{Conditions}
Names below are used in every table. \textbf{Core conditions.}
\texttt{blank} is the model-free backbone alone; \texttt{blank64} is the
backbone with every update built from 64 distinct real transitions, each
repeated once to fill the batch, the real-data content of a Dyna update and no
model; with a mean-reduced loss this is SAC at batch 64;
\texttt{prior} adds the frozen body model; \texttt{coach} adds the frozen
teacher in the additive regime. \textbf{Adversarial controls.}
\texttt{randprior} replaces the babble-trained model with a randomly
initialised frozen model in identical Dyna machinery; \texttt{randcoach}
replaces the trained teacher with a randomly initialised one distilled through
the same loss at the same weight. \texttt{priorcoach} carries both aids.

\textbf{Withdrawing the teacher} at step $t_w$: \texttt{none} (no teacher,
identical to \texttt{blank}); \texttt{constant} ($c=1$ throughout, identical
to \texttt{coach}); \texttt{abrupt} ($c\to0$ at $t_w$, the literature's
withdrawal); \texttt{selfanchor} (at $t_w$ the teacher is replaced by a frozen
copy of the student's own actor, $c$ unchanged: the term survives with the
same weight and form but carries no external information); \texttt{randanchor}
(replaced by a freshly initialised actor); and, in the replacing regime only,
\texttt{gateon} ($q\to1$ at $t_w$ with the real teacher kept). The literature's
measure of dependence is $\text{abrupt}-\text{none}$, the withdrawn learner
against one never guided. Three paired contrasts attribute the cost:
$\text{abrupt}-\text{constant}$ is losing the teacher \emph{and} the loss term
(the total), $\text{selfanchor}-\text{constant}$ is losing the teacher's
information only (the teacher component), and
$\text{abrupt}-\text{selfanchor}$ is losing the loss term only (the loss-term
component). The snapshot holds whatever of the teacher the student had
absorbed by $t_w$, so the teacher component prices the teacher's
\emph{further} information. Since \texttt{none} and \texttt{constant} are
shared across $t_w$, $\text{abrupt}-\text{none}$ and the total have the same
trend with attachment. Every withdrawal is a single step.

\textbf{Withdrawing the body model} at step 8000: \texttt{keep}
never withdraws (identical to \texttt{prior}); \texttt{soft} stops generating
imagined transitions but leaves the imagined buffer in place, so updates keep
drawing 64 real and 64 stale imagined; \texttt{purge} also empties the buffer,
after which every batch is 128 real, the strict withdrawal, which removes
imagination and doubles distinct real transitions per update at once;
\texttt{matched} withdraws as \texttt{purge} does but draws 64 real
transitions and repeats them, holding distinct real transitions per update at
64. Only contrasts among \texttt{keep}, \texttt{soft} and \texttt{matched} are
single-factor.

\subsection{Metrics and statistics}
\emph{Endpoint}: held-out error at 100k. \emph{Dip}: error at $t_w{+}1000$
minus error at $t_w$. \emph{Attachment trend}: per-seed ordinary-least-squares
slope of a component against $t_w$, then a paired-$t$ interval across seeds,
per 1{,}000 attached steps. The seed is the unit of analysis; every condition
has $n=12$ matched seeds except the three- and four-joint arms, which have
$n=24$; every comparison is a direct paired contrast on the seed, never an
inference from two separate verdicts; intervals are paired-$t$ 95\% intervals
with $\mathrm{df}=n-1$; an interval excluding zero is called significant and
marked~$^*$ or set in bold; a non-significant cell is a failure to detect at
this power, never evidence of equivalence. Three endpoints were fixed in
advance for the converged grid, 12k, 50k and 100k; counts over the fifty
2k-checkpoints are reported both uncorrected and after a Holm correction over
the fifty tests, and are descriptive. Every significant contrast in
Tables~\ref{tab:conv} and \ref{tab:decomp} was also checked with an exact
Wilcoxon signed-rank test; the two it does not confirm at $p<0.05$ are flagged
where they appear. Tables~\ref{tab:arms}, \ref{tab:sweep}, \ref{tab:replace}
and \ref{tab:withdraw} rest on the $t$ interval. Convergence is checked per
condition as the paired change over the last 20{,}000 steps; of the 97
conditions in the tables, five have an interval excluding zero and are named
where they occur.

\subsection{Implementation details}
Actor and critics are two-layer 128-unit SiLU networks, the twin critics with
layer normalisation; $\gamma=0.99$, Polyak $\tau=0.005$, Adam at
$3\times10^{-4}$, target entropy $-0.5\,|\mathcal{A}|$, gradient norm clipped
at 10, real replay 200{,}000 transitions, imagined replay 400{,}000, 1{,}000
warm-up steps, update-to-data ratio 2, batch 128. Dyna: every 100 environment
steps, 200 start states are drawn from real replay and rolled $k=3$ steps under
the current actor through the frozen model, with the environment's analytic
reward evaluated at the imagined states. The forward model is a two-layer
256-unit SiLU network predicting the next state, trained with Adam at
$10^{-3}$ for 40 epochs on babble data collected with Ornstein--Uhlenbeck
activations ($\theta=0.15$, $\sigma=0.4$, clipped to $[-1,1]$, pose
re-randomised every 100 steps, no reward). Teachers have the actor's
architecture. Evaluation is one greedy 100-step episode on each of the twenty
held-out targets, every 2{,}000 steps, and every 1{,}000 in the withdrawal
conditions. A matched seed sets the network initialisation, replay sampling,
the training environment and the order of target draws; the targets are fixed
across seeds, and runs are deterministic on CPU. The 1{,}500 runs behind the tables took about 1{,}500 single-thread
CPU-hours.

\subsection{Reproduction}
An earlier, unpublished version of this study scored every condition at 12k
with the same code. Every one of its runs with a counterpart here is
reproduced to the digit at the shared checkpoints (core grid, both body-model
withdrawals, elbow attachment sweep, replacing regime, anchor conditions);
dips that need a 1k evaluation grid are read from these reproduced runs, and
the budget check uses the 12k runs as they are.

\subsection{What the priors are worth at convergence}
\label{sec:conv}
Table~\ref{tab:conv} and Fig.~\ref{fig:conv} score the core conditions on the
two MyoSuite bodies at 100{,}000 steps; every condition in the table has
plateaued by the paired-change criterion except the elbow's random-model
control, still falling at \ci{-24.99}{-43.78}{-6.19}.

\textbf{The body model.} On the elbow its advantage over model-free learning,
\ci{-20.72}{-35.85}{-5.59}~mrad at 12k, is \ci{-4.09}{-11.11}{+2.94} at 100k,
not detected; it is significant at seven of fifty checkpoints uncorrected and
one after the Holm correction. On the finger the advantage, not detected at
12k (\ci{-27.10}{-66.32}{+12.12}~mm), is \ci{-40.29}{-57.95}{-22.64} at 100k
and is detected at every checkpoint from 14k on (45 of 50 uncorrected, 42
corrected). The \texttt{blank64} condition prices the real-data half of the
Dyna update: halving the real transitions per update costs the elbow
\ci{+9.68}{-3.69}{+23.06}, not detected, and \emph{helps} the finger by
\ci{-14.59}{-26.00}{-3.18}, as smaller batches are known to help off-policy
learners by reducing critic overfitting \cite{rybkin2025,nauman2024,obandoceron2023}.
Against this matched control, and against a random model, the babble model
keeps its advantage (Table~\ref{tab:conv}).

Table~\ref{tab:arms} and Fig.~\ref{fig:arms} extend the body-model contrasts
to four planar arms of one to four joints, with twenty-four seeds on the two
hardest. The model beats model-free learning on the one-, two- and four-joint
arms and is not detected on the three-joint arm; it beats the random model on
every arm, and the random model's harm relative to no model grows
monotonically with joint count, from not detected on one joint to
\ci{+257.72}{+177.95}{+337.48}~mm on four (per-seed slope of the relative harm,
\ci{+0.41}{+0.18}{+0.63} of the model-free error per joint, exact Wilcoxon
$p=0.003$). The model's own relative advantage does not grow with joint count
detectably (slope \ci{-0.10}{-0.25}{+0.05} per joint, $p=0.18$). Against
\texttt{blank64} it is detected on the one-, two- and four-joint arms, though
the four-joint \texttt{blank64} condition again sits below \texttt{blank}
(\ci{-62.42}{-129.04}{+4.20}). One condition in the table is still moving at
100k, the one-joint \texttt{randprior}, rising (\ci{+13.80}{+3.54}{+24.06}).

\textbf{The teacher.} On the elbow the coached student keeps a small advantage
over model-free learning at 100k, \ci{-8.06}{-15.83}{-0.28}, detected by the
$t$ interval at 43 of 50 checkpoints uncorrected and 13 after correction, but
with an exact Wilcoxon $p=0.052$ at the endpoint; its lead over the body model,
\ci{-10.89}{-19.26}{-2.52} at 12k, is \ci{-3.97}{-8.47}{+0.53} at 100k. On the
finger the teacher decides everything. With T1, a student held near a policy
that barely moves does not improve past 12k (122.77~mm at 12k, 123.00 at
100k), ends \ci{+28.33}{+16.11}{+40.56} worse than model-free learning,
\ci{+68.63}{+59.19}{+78.07} worse than the body model, and
\ci{+13.86}{+7.62}{+20.09} worse than a student held near a random teacher.
With T2 on the same body and seeds the coached student ends at 34.50~mm, level
with T2 plus the body model and better than every condition without T2
(Table~\ref{tab:conv}), within 1.1~mm of its teacher (35.6). The ordering of the two routes is
therefore a property of the teacher. A fixed 12k endpoint would have detected
the elbow body-model benefit and missed the finger's.

\textbf{Budget.} The body model saw 3.3$\times$ (elbow) to 6.7$\times$
(finger) more transitions than T1. Rebuilding the finger model from exactly
30k babble transitions changes its student's 12k endpoint by
\ci{+2.75}{-12.77}{+18.27}~mm, and a 30k elbow model changes it by
\ci{+1.55}{-0.87}{+3.97}~mrad, neither detected. T2 is not matched to anything:
it is the product of $2\times10^5$ babble transitions and 100k rewarded steps.

The additive coach is the wrong model of physical guidance, which substitutes
for the learner's own error correction rather than supplementing it. The
replacing regime is the right one, and it is also what reward-free
distillation does in current legged-locomotion pipelines
\cite{lee2020,miki2022}. We ran it on both bodies at
$t_w\in\{2000,4000,6000,8000\}$ with \texttt{constant}, \texttt{abrupt},
\texttt{selfanchor} and \texttt{gateon} to 100k (Table~\ref{tab:replace}).
The bodies differ in what the collapse at withdrawal is and agree that it does
not last.

On the elbow, four thousand steps after withdrawal the hypothesis appears
confirmed: the cost of losing the teacher is large at every $t_w$ and grows
with attachment ($\text{selfanchor}-\text{constant}$ slope
\ci{+5.77}{+1.30}{+10.25}), an order of magnitude above the additive regime.
The \texttt{gateon} condition dissolves it: keeping the real teacher while
switching on the student's own gradient produces the same collapse, seed by
seed ($\text{selfanchor}-\text{gateon}$ within 5.0~mrad at every $t_w$, no
slope). What grows is the cost of first activating the learner's own policy
gradient after a cloning-only phase, the known collapse when a cloned policy is
fine-tuned by off-policy reinforcement \cite{nair2020,uchendu2023,zhang2023}.
On the finger the collapse is the deleted term instead, as in the additive
regime: plain SAC after withdrawal is 59 to 93~mm above the never-withdrawn
student four thousand steps later, while keeping the teacher costs 3 to 7~mm,
as a retained cloning term is reported to do \cite{zhao2022}, and nothing grows
with attachment ($\text{abrupt}-\text{constant}$ slope
\ci{-2.19}{-5.78}{+1.40}).

By 100k the two bodies agree. Plain SAC after withdrawal ends not
distinguishably from a learner never guided on the elbow
($\text{abrupt}-\text{none}$ $+1.0$ to $+3.2$, none detected, slope
\ci{+0.22}{-0.65}{+1.09}) and 38 to 59~mm ahead of it on the finger, by more
the longer the teacher was attached (slope \ci{-3.20}{-4.91}{-1.50}); it is
4.0 to 6.2~mrad above the never-withdrawn student on the elbow (detected at
$t_w\ge4000$) and above it on the finger only after the earliest withdrawal.
Keeping the teacher leaves the learner 2 to 5~mrad or mm ahead of the
never-withdrawn student on both bodies. The pure-distillation student is level
with model-free learning on the elbow (\ci{-3.02}{-9.59}{+3.55}) and far ahead
of it on the finger (\ci{-58.32}{-71.38}{-45.26}). One condition is still
moving at 100k, the elbow \texttt{gateon} at $t_w=6000$
(\ci{-2.75}{-5.16}{-0.34}), downward. After a cloning-only phase the student's
snapshot approximates the teacher, so \texttt{selfanchor} cannot price the
teacher's information in this regime; on the finger a snapshot taken at
$t_w=2000$, before the student had cloned its teacher, is the one lasting cost
(\ci{+20.72}{+14.50}{+26.94} against \texttt{constant}).

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{What dependence looks like when it can be taken apart.}
Informationless stand-ins for guidance are rare in human studies, so dependence
is measured as the withdrawn learner against one never guided, and growth with
attachment is read as accumulated reliance. The finger reproduces that growth
at the immediate drop, and the self-anchor attributes it: the teacher
component falls with attachment on both bodies, because it tracks what the
teacher was still contributing, and the deleted term's shock is what grows.
Whether an optimisation event that grows with attachment should be called
dependence is a matter of definition; what remains at convergence is not.
There the withdrawn learner is never detectably worse than one never guided,
and on the finger it is better by more the longer it was guided, the reverse of
the hypothesis. On the elbow what persists is 3 to 9~mrad of a competent
teacher's further information, of the size of the teacher's whole advantage and
flat in attachment. The body model behaves alike: its withdrawal leaves the
finger learner 15~mm short of one that kept it and 25~mm ahead of one that
never had it. In the replacing regime on the elbow the cost that grows with
attachment belongs to an actor that has never been moved by its critic, the
hypothesis' mechanism in a form the additive regime cannot show; on the finger
the collapse is the deleted term and does not grow; on both it is gone by
convergence. Our evaluation is always greedy and without the teacher, so every
checkpoint is a no-guidance transfer test, and our dip follows continued
practice, the analogue of a delayed retention test with practice in between
rather than the no-practice test the guidance literature favours
\cite{winstein1990}. Human studies do find withdrawal costs at retention after
fixed practice \cite{winstein1994,sullivan2008}, and a field study with our
yardstick found students whose tutor was withdrawn below students who never had
one \cite{bastani2025}. Our claim is about the state after convergence, where a
weaned learner can end above a from-scratch one \cite{schmitt2018} and a
regulariser removed after the early phase costs little \cite{golatkar2019},
though early experience can also leave lasting marks on a deep RL learner
\cite{nikishin2022}. Here it did not.

\textbf{What the priors are worth.} A frozen babble model is worth something on
four of six bodies and harmful on none, and what it is worth is its content: a
random model in the same machinery harms by more the more joints there are, a
series the model's own benefit does not follow, so a harder body makes a wrong
model cost more rather than a right one worth more. A teacher is worth its
competence: with a near-no-op and a competent teacher the same body, seeds and
loss give the worst finger condition and one level with the best, so the two
routes cannot be ordered on one body with one teacher.

\textbf{Measurement.} A withdrawal that changes two things at once cannot be
read as the cost of the thing withdrawn: the body model needed a matched-replay
condition, the teacher a self-anchor, and a cloning-only learner a
\texttt{gateon} condition, since a distillation learner with no reward term
\cite{lee2020,miki2022} freezes when its coefficient is zeroed. Fading
schedules deserve the same decomposition, since a taper may reduce the shock
rather than the dependence. And an endpoint fixed in advance sat inside a
transient on both MyoSuite bodies; the remedy is a per-condition plateau check,
which here leaves five of 97 conditions moving.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
The study is single-task reach in simulation, on deterministic, fully observed
bodies, which favour a frozen forward model; the one-joint bodies' held-out
targets interpolate between the training targets. The finger's first teacher
had drifted to within 3.2~mm of a zero-activation policy, so its results concern
distilling toward a near-no-op target. The self-anchor carries what the student
had absorbed by withdrawal, so the fall of the teacher component with attachment
is partly by construction; the convergence claim rests on
$\text{abrupt}-\text{none}$, which needs no anchor. The body-model condition is
handed the analytic reward at imagined states. Withdrawal is a single step, attachment was varied over seven
points, and contrasts were not pre-registered. No physical robot is involved.

\section{Conclusion}
Two routes to a motor prior were compared under one learner on six
muscle-driven bodies, to a checked plateau, with adversarial controls and
single-factor withdrawals. What a teacher still contributes at withdrawal falls
with attachment; what grows, where anything does, is the shock of deleting its
loss term. At convergence a learner that lost a teacher or a body model is never
behind one that never had it; what can remain is a shortfall against keeping
the prior. What the priors were worth is bound to their content: a babble model
helps on four of six bodies, a random one harms by more the more joints there
are, and a teacher is worth its competence.
